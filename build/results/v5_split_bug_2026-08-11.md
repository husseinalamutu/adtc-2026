# v5 / v5b / v5c — a data-split bug, and two retrains spent on the wrong theory

> Supersedes the dilution analysis in `v5_dilution_finding_2026-08-11.md`, which reached a
> confident and **incorrect** conclusion. That file is kept for the audit trail; this one
> is the correct account.

## The observation
| Run | Nigeria oversample | Facts | Arithmetic | Narration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **v3 (shipped)** | 3× | **34/37** | 9/12 | 5/5 |
| v5 | 2× | 26/37 | 10/12 | 4–5/5 |
| v5b | 4× | 27/37 | 11/12 | 5/5 |
| v5c | 3×, data restored | pending | pending | pending |

## The wrong theory (cost: one full retrain)
v5's fact collapse was read as **gradient dilution** — Nigeria's share of the mix had fallen
from ~48% (v3) to ~26% (v5) because 1,100 new drills were added while oversampling dropped
3×→2×. The prediction was that restoring exposure would restore facts.

**v5b falsified it.** Doubling exposure to 4× recovered exactly one question (26→27). If
share were the mechanism, the recovery should have been most of the eight-question gap.

## The actual cause
The topic breakdown had been pointing at it the whole time:

| Topic | v3 | v5 | Δ |
|---|---|---|---|
| development_levy | 4/4 | 1/4 | −3 |
| personal_income_tax | 3/3 | 1/3 | −2 |
| withholding_tax | 1/2 | 0/2 | −1 |
| *(everything else)* | | | −2 |

Those three topics are **six of the eight lost questions** — and inspecting the split showed
why:

```
holdout nigeria families (v5/v5b):
  nigeria_tax_drill::development_levy    103 rows
  nigeria_tax_drill::personal_income_tax  66 rows
  nigeria_tax_drill::withholding_tax      57 rows
```

`build_dataset.py` splits **by scenario family**. Adding the new `arith::*` and `intel::*`
families changed the family list, the shuffle landed differently, and those three drill
families were assigned **wholesale to holdout**. The model saw *zero* training examples for
those facts. In the v3 dataset all three were in train, at nearly identical counts.

**You cannot oversample data that is not in the training set.** That is why v5b's 4× did
nothing: it was multiplying the wrong rows.

## The fix (`build_dataset.py`)
1. **Fact-drill families are pinned to train and can never be held out.** Holding out a
   *scenario* family tests generalisation to an unseen situation — that is the point of the
   split. Holding out a *fact drill* family deletes the fact from training; a model cannot
   recall a rate it was never shown. The split conflated "unseen situation" with
   "unseen fact".
2. **Holdout is stratified by category.** The first version of fix (1) overcorrected and
   left holdout with zero Nigeria rows, silently voiding the overfit guard the REPORT
   claims. Every category with more than one eligible family now contributes to holdout.

Result: Nigeria training rows 642 → 854, all 8 drill families present, holdout covers all
7 categories. 139 data tests pass.

## What was actually learned
- **The arithmetic drills work, independent of the bug.** 9 → 10 → 11/12 across three runs
  while the fact data was broken. Deterministic ground truth plus showing the working is a
  reliable technique.
- **A confident diagnosis is not a verified one.** The dilution theory was plausible,
  quantified, and wrong. The topic-level breakdown contained the disproof from the start
  (the loss was concentrated in three topics, not spread across all of them) — a share
  effect would degrade broadly, not surgically. That distinction was available before the
  v5b run and was not checked.
- **Check the data before re-running the model.** One retrain (~1h) was spent on a theory
  that a two-minute inspection of the split would have ruled out.
- **The gates did their job three times.** v4, v5 and v5b were all prevented from shipping.
  Every one of them had a validation loss that looked fine — v5's was the best of any run.

## Process change adopted
Any future fact regression is now diagnosed in this order:
1. Is the training data actually present? (`category`/family counts per split)
2. Is the eval contaminated or changed?
3. Only then consider mix, capacity or over-training.
